Simplifying surds

Problem 1: Express \( \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \) with a rational denominator.

Solution: CLICK TO REVEAL

"Rational" numbers are ones that can be expressed as fractions of whole numbers, like \( \tfrac{-7}{4} \).
Surds like \( \sqrt{2} \) aren't rational unless the number inside is a perfect square.
So the problem is to get rid of the surd in the denominator (bottom part) of the fraction.
Recall that we can change a fraction to an "equivalent fraction" by multiplying the top and bottom by the same thing. This doesn't change its value, because it's basically multiplying by 1.
In this case, we multiply the top and bottom of the fraction by \( \sqrt{3} \)

\( \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \quad = \quad \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \times \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}} \quad = \quad \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3} \)

The result is arguably not as simple as the original expression, but there are reasons we prefer it. Mainly, as we shall see, it enables us to group like surds that we might not otherwise have recognised.

Problem 2: Simplify \( \sqrt{2} + \sqrt{8} + \sqrt{18} \).

Solution: CLICK TO REVEAL

This is basically an exercise in grouping 'like' terms, which means ones containing the same surds.
But in this case none of the surds appear to match.
This is why it's important to simplify each surd by taking out square factors.

\( \sqrt{8} \quad = \quad \sqrt{4 \times 2} \quad = \quad \sqrt{4} \times \sqrt{2} \quad = \quad 2\sqrt{2} \)

Similarly...

\( \sqrt{18} \quad = \quad \sqrt{9 \times 2} \quad = \quad \sqrt{9} \times \sqrt{2} \quad = \quad 3\sqrt{2} \)

Suddenly our non-matching surds are matching surds...

\( \sqrt{2} + \sqrt{8} + \sqrt{18} \quad = \quad \sqrt{2} + 2\sqrt{2} + 3\sqrt{2} \quad = \quad 6\sqrt{3} \)

Problem 3: Simplify \( \tfrac{15}{8} ( \sqrt{5} + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}} + \sqrt{20} ) \)

Solution: CLICK TO REVEAL

Putting together the techniques from the first two problems...

\( \tfrac{15}{8} ( \sqrt{5} + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}} + \sqrt{20} ) \\ \quad = \tfrac{15}{8} ( \sqrt{5} + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \times \tfrac{\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{5}} + \sqrt{4} \times \sqrt{5} ) \\ \quad = \tfrac{15}{8} ( \sqrt{5} + \tfrac{\sqrt{5}}{5} + 2 \sqrt{5} ) \\ \quad = \tfrac{15}{8} ( 1 + \tfrac{1}{5} + 2 ) \sqrt{5} \\ \text{ converting terms to a common denominator so we can add them... } \\ \quad = \tfrac{15}{8} ( \tfrac{5}{5} + \tfrac{1}{5} + \tfrac{10}{5} ) \sqrt{5} \\ \quad = \tfrac{15}{8} ( \tfrac{16}{5} ) \sqrt{5} \\ \quad = 6 \sqrt{5} \\ \)
